## Your research question (RQ2, attribution)
"Is the effect of multi-factor authentication due to combining factors or to the strength of one of them? What does the password contribute once a cryptographic key is present, and what does a second factor contribute beside a strong, unique password?"
The protocol predicts: against remote threats the effect comes from the strongest factor (beside a service-bound cryptographic key the password adds nothing), while against local threats a second, locally verified factor does add protection. That prediction may be wrong. Your job is NOT to confirm it. Find the strongest evidence for "one strong factor does the work" AND the strongest evidence for "the combination matters" (factor independence, defence in depth, cases where the password or PIN beside a key stopped an attack, key extraction or cloning, device theft, malware, synced passkeys, downgrade to a weaker factor). Also establish from the standards how they classify a cryptographic key unlocked by PIN or biometric (single- or multi-factor) and a key without user verification, and the reasons they give. If either pile ends up empty, the report must show the queries that came back empty. Direct evidence may be scarce (few studies isolate one factor's contribution): say plainly which conclusions rest on measurement, which on attack demonstrations, and which only on analysis.
